\title[CascadeRAG: abstention-triggered escalation for retrieval-augmented generation]{CascadeRAG: Abstention-Triggered Escalation that Outperforms Costlier Retrieval-Augmented Generation Architectures at a Fraction of their Cost}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Miko{\l}aj} \sur{Semeniuk}}\email{m.semeniuk@icloud.com}
\author[1]{\fnm{Agnieszka} \sur{Jastrz{\k{e}}bska}}\email{agnieszka.jastrzebska@mini.pw.edu.pl}

\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{Faculty of Mathematics and Information Science}, \orgname{Warsaw University of Technology}, \orgaddress{\street{Koszykowa 75}, \city{Warsaw}, \postcode{00-662}, \country{Poland}}}

% The journal allows 150-250 words here; keep it under 250.
\abstract{Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) grounds a language model's
answers in an external document collection, and many architectural
refinements (hybrid retrieval, cross-encoder reranking, hypothetical
document embeddings, corrective and iterative retrieval, routing) compete
for a place in practical systems. Their published evaluations are rarely
comparable, because each uses its own corpus, retriever, generator, and
context budget. We present a controlled comparison of thirteen RAG
architectures on a single stack (one 21-million-passage corpus, one
embedding model, one generator, one prompt) over five question sets and
more than 37{,}000 questions, with paired significance tests under
multiplicity control, budget-matched controls, abstention measured
separately from error, and retrieval read against the measured ceiling
the corpus imposes. No refinement is universally safe: cross-encoder
reranking gains $+0.051$ Exact Match on one question set and loses
$0.100$ on another. A per-question diagnosis of why retrieval fails
explains these reversals and yields a novel design, CascadeRAG: retrieve
with a fusion of dense, lexical, and cross-encoder rankings, and escalate
to a costlier strategy only when the reader itself, having seen the
evidence, declines to answer, a posterior signal that is provably
lossless under Exact Match. Across \ResultPairs{} paired comparisons
CascadeRAG wins \ResultWins{}, loses \ResultLosses{}, and ties
\ResultTies{}, at 1.16--1.58 generator calls per question against
2.3--3.0 for iterative and corrective methods, and the result replicates
under a second generator. Two negative results are documented:
neighbour-chunk expansion adds nothing once context size is controlled,
and answer-token confidence adds nothing over abstention. Every number is
generated from the released per-question logs.}

\keywords{retrieval-augmented generation, large language models, open-domain question answering, selective prediction, cascade architectures, evaluation methodology}
