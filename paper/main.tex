\title{CascadeRAG: Abstention-Triggered Escalation\\
That Outperforms Costlier Retrieval-Augmented Generation\\
Architectures at a Fraction of Their Cost}
\author{Miko{\l}aj Semeniuk \and Agnieszka Jastrz{\k{e}}bska}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) grounds a language model's answers in
an external document collection, and a large family of architectural
refinements --- hybrid retrieval, cross-encoder reranking, hypothetical
document embeddings, corrective retrieval, iterative retrieval, routing
--- competes for a place in practical systems. Published evaluations of
these refinements are rarely comparable: each uses its own corpus,
retriever, generator, and context budget. We present a controlled
comparison of thirteen RAG architectures on a single stack --- one
21-million-passage corpus, one embedding model, one generator, one answer
prompt --- over five question sets and more than 37{,}000 questions, with
paired significance tests under multiplicity control, budget-matched
controls, abstention measured separately from error, and retrieval read
against the ceiling the corpus imposes, which we measure. No refinement is
universally safe: the strongest one, cross-encoder reranking, gains
$+0.051$ Exact Match on one question set and loses $-0.100$ on another. A
per-question diagnosis of \emph{why} retrieval fails explains these
reversals and yields a design we call CascadeRAG: retrieve with a fusion
of dense, lexical, and cross-encoder rankings, and escalate to a more
expensive strategy only when the reader itself, having seen the evidence,
declines to answer --- a posterior signal that is provably lossless under
Exact Match, so escalation cannot discard a correct answer. Across
\ResultPairs{} paired (baseline, question set) comparisons CascadeRAG
wins \ResultWins{},
loses \ResultLosses{}, and ties \ResultTies{}, at 1.16--1.58 generator
calls per question against 2.3--3.0 for iterative and corrective methods,
and the result replicates under a second, unrelated generator. Two
negative results are documented along the way: neighbour-chunk expansion
adds nothing once context size is controlled, and answer-token confidence
adds nothing over the abstention signal it accompanies. Every number in
the article is generated from the per-question logs by the released
benchmark pipeline.
\end{abstract}
